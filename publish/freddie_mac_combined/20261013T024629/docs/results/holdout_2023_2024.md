## Interpretation

1. **Rank-ordering generalises well.** All models reach AUC 0.81–0.82 on loans with first payments 7–10 years after the
   training cohorts. That is higher than on the 2022–23 test (0.74–0.77), where disaster-related delinquency was
   heavier.
2. **The LightGBM challenger adds nothing material.** M4 beats M1 by +0.004 AUC with heavily overlapping CIs. This
   matches the development finding that M4's gain was not statistically distinguishable from zero. For rank-ordering,
   the simpler logistic model is as good.
3. **Calibration fails in opposite directions, as the note-rate mechanism predicts.** 2023–25 note rates
   average 6.6–6.8% (5th–95th percentile 5.6–7.9%), and **about 90% of holdout loans carry a rate above the training
   maximum (5.875%)**.
   - The **logistic** model extrapolates the rate coefficient and **over-predicts** (observed/expected 0.62).
   - **Trees** cannot extrapolate beyond the training range, so M4 behaves like the drifting baseline and
     **under-predicts** (1.78).

   Neither is fit for PD-level use under the pre-registered band [0.8, 1.25].
4. **Implication:** absolute PDs need **recalibration on the most recent mature cohorts** (or explicit macro terms).
   Features or algorithm choice alone do not fix level drift.

All three are eligible for **rank-ordering demonstrations only**, after a named reviewer records a promotion.
Recommendation: prefer **M1 (logistic)** for demonstrations. It ranks risk as well as M4, is simpler to explain and
audit, and gives exact additive reason codes. Pair it with a recalibration step before any PD-level use.

## Limitations

Provider sample (50,000 loans per vintage); loan-level bootstrap CIs understate cohort-level uncertainty; funded,
Freddie Mac-acquired loans only; the reason codes are attributions, not adverse-action reasons; `property_state`
proxy review is still pending.
